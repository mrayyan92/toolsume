What Is WebP Format and Why Does It Exist?

WebP is a modern image format developed by Google in 2010 and released as an open-source project. The format was specifically designed to provide superior compression for images on the web, reducing file sizes by as much as 25 to 34 percent compared to JPEG and PNG formats while maintaining visually comparable quality. WebP supports both lossy and lossless compression, which means it can handle photographic images like JPG and also graphics with transparency like PNG. It even supports animation, making it a versatile alternative to GIF as well.

Since its introduction, WebP has gained massive adoption across the internet. Major platforms like Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge, and Safari now support WebP natively. Google services including Google Search, Google Ads, and Google Play Store use WebP extensively. Content management systems like WordPress, Shopify, and Squarespace serve WebP images by default to optimize page load speed. Major e-commerce platforms and social media networks have also embraced the format to deliver faster experiences to billions of users worldwide.

Despite its widespread adoption, WebP is not universally supported in all software applications, operating systems, and workflows. This is precisely why tools like the ToolHub WebP to JPG converter have become indispensable for everyday users and professionals alike. Understanding the strengths and limitations of WebP helps you make informed decisions about when to use it natively and when converting to JPG is the better choice.

Why Convert WebP to JPG? Understanding the Need

There are several compelling reasons why converting WebP to JPG remains a popular and necessary task. The first and most obvious reason is compatibility. While modern web browsers fully support WebP, many desktop applications, mobile apps, email clients, and cloud storage services still do not recognize the format. Microsoft Paint, certain versions of Adobe Photoshop without a plugin, older image viewers on Windows, and many email signature editors simply cannot open WebP files. Converting to JPG eliminates these compatibility headaches instantly.

Print readiness is another major factor. Most print shops, photo labs, and professional printing services require JPG or TIFF files. If you have WebP images that you want to print as physical photographs, posters, business cards, or marketing materials, converting them to JPG is a mandatory first step. The JPG format has been the standard for printed media for decades, and virtually every printer and print workflow supports it without any issues.

Social media and messaging platforms also play a role. While many platforms now accept WebP uploads, some still convert them automatically or handle them inconsistently. Uploading a JPG ensures your image appears exactly as intended across Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, Twitter, WhatsApp, and email without any unexpected format conversions or quality losses imposed by the platform.

Editing workflows present another common scenario. Many photo editing applications, including older versions of Lightroom, GIMP, CorelDRAW, and various mobile editors, work best with JPG files. Professional photographers and designers who need to batch-edit, apply filters, or composite images often prefer working with JPG because of its universal support across all editing tools.

Finally, file size considerations sometimes make JPG the preferred choice. While WebP generally produces smaller files, there are edge cases where JPG with a specific quality setting produces a file that better suits your storage, bandwidth, or upload requirements. Having the ability to convert between formats gives you complete control over your image assets.

WebP vs JPG vs PNG: Choosing the Right Format

Understanding when to use each format helps you make better decisions about your image assets. WebP is ideal for web use when file size reduction and modern compression are priorities. It produces the smallest files with the best quality, making it perfect for websites, apps, and digital content where loading speed matters.

JPG remains the universal standard for photographic images. It is supported by virtually every device, application, and platform ever made. Use JPG when you need maximum compatibility, when working with print workflows, or when sharing images across diverse systems and applications.

PNG is the format of choice when you need lossless compression and transparency.
